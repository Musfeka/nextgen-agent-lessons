Teaching workspace

Agent Skills

Ten units, from "what is an agent" to a Skill you actually use.

For learning by building, then delivering to a cross-functional team Grounded in Anthropic primary sources

Work through the units in order — each assumes the one before. Every unit ends with an activity that feeds the next, and all of them feed the Unit 10 capstone. The reference documents are the compressed versions: print those, not the lessons.

The learning progression

Understand→ Observe→ Decompose→ Design→ Build→ Test→ Evaluate→ Compose→ Demonstrate

  • RESOURCES.md — the trusted sources, annotated, with gaps stated explicitly.
  • GLOSSARY.md — canonical terms, promoted when demonstrated rather than when taught.
  • NOTES.md — teaching decisions, preferences, and open questions.
  • learning-records/ — what has actually been established, and what it unlocks.

Two things to know before you start

Where this material is synthesis rather than spec

Units 1–6 and 8 teach documented behaviour, cited throughout. Unit 7 (composition / SkillGraph) and Unit 9 (role-specific patterns) are original synthesis — there is no authoritative source for either. Both units say so on the page. Keep that labelling when you re-deliver this: it costs nothing and protects your credibility on everything else.

A rollout decision to make before the team session

Skills do not sync between claude.ai, the API and Claude Code — and on claude.ai custom Skills are per-user with no admin distribution. For 21 people that means 21 diverging copies within a quarter unless you choose a canonical home first: a git repository with Claude Code's project directory, or an API workspace. Details in Unit 10.

I am your teacher for this course. That is what I am here for, and it is where the most learning happens.
