What kind of unit this is

Synthesis. No primary source covers role-specific Skill patterns. Everything below applies the documented authoring principles to your six role families. Treat the candidates as well-reasoned starting points, not as received wisdom — and expect your own practitioners to improve on them immediately. That is the intended outcome.

Twenty-one job titles, six families. Rather than twenty-one tracks, the useful move is to notice that every family has the same shape of first Skill — and that the highest-value candidate is almost never the most obvious one.

9.1 The pattern that repeats across all six

Before the lists, the generalisation worth teaching. In every profession, the best first Skill is the one that applies your standards to somebody else's work product — not the one that generates from scratch.

Tempting first Skill: generate

"Write the test cases." "Draft the proposal." "Create the personas."

Hard to evaluate, high stakes, and the model was already decent at it. Improvement is difficult to demonstrate.

Better first Skill: apply standards

"Check these test cases against our coverage rules." "Review this proposal against our qualification criteria."

Checkable, low risk, and encodes knowledge only your team has. Ground truth is available.

This maps directly to Unit 8: review work has natural evaluation criteria. Generation work does not. Start where you can tell whether it worked.

A note for the designers in the room

The 3D and UX roles are where people most often assume "this is not for me." The opposite is true: design teams carry an unusual amount of undocumented standards, and undocumented standards are exactly what Skills capture. The asset-specification review above is one of the most deterministically checkable Skills on this entire page.

9.3 A worked example, deliberately not from engineering

To make the point that this is not a developer tool:

qualifying-rfps/SKILL.md 
---
name: qualifying-rfps
description: Assesses an incoming RFP, tender or proposal request against the
  firm's capability list and commercial thresholds, and returns a bid / no-bid
  recommendation with the reasoning. Use when a new RFP, tender, ITT or client
  proposal request arrives and someone needs to decide whether to pursue it.
---

# Qualifying RFPs

You are applying the qualification standards of a Senior BD Executive.
Be decisive. A soft "maybe" is the least useful output.

## Process
1. Extract every mandatory requirement into a list.
2. Mark each: CAN DELIVER / PARTIAL / CANNOT — using capabilities.md only.
3. Extract commercial terms: value, timeline, payment, penalties.
4. Apply the thresholds below.
5. Give a recommendation with the three strongest reasons.

## Thresholds
- Any CANNOT on a mandatory requirement → recommend NO BID.
- More than two PARTIALs → recommend NO BID unless the value
  exceeds the standard threshold.
- Timeline under six weeks from award → flag as high risk.

## Rules
- MUST NOT claim a capability that is not listed in capabilities.md.
- MUST list every requirement you could not classify. Never guess.
- If commercial terms are absent, say so — do not assume standard terms.

## Output
1. Recommendation: BID / NO BID / BID WITH CONDITIONS
2. Three reasons, most decisive first
3. Compliance table: Requirement | Status | Evidence
4. Unclassified requirements needing a human

Note what makes this work: capabilities.md sits alongside as a bundled file (level 3), so the capability list can be updated without touching the Skill. The "MUST NOT claim a capability not listed" rule is the failure handling from Unit 6, doing the job that matters most.
