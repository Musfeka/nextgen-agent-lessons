Build Your Own Agent Skill

Can I take one repetitive task from my week and turn it into something reusable?

Time 90 minutes, spread over a week You will produce a working, installed, evaluated Skill and a before/after demo Prerequisites Units 1–9

Everything so far has been preparation for this. The deliverable is not knowledge about Skills — it is a Skill, installed, tested against real inputs, with evidence that it changed the work. Work through the nine steps in order; each one has a checklist you can tick and a place to put your answer.

The standard to hold yourself to

You are done when a colleague can use your Skill on their input, without asking you a question, and get output you would be willing to sign. Not when the file exists.

Step 1 · Identify the task

Return to your Unit 1 list and your Unit 4 tool map. Score each candidate honestly:

Five ticks means build it. Three or fewer means pick a different candidate — you will learn more from a small win than a stalled ambitious one.

Step 2 · Decompose it

Input → activities → decisions → output, as in Unit 2. Be specific about where judgement happens, because that is what you are encoding.

Step 3 · Establish your baseline — do not skip

This is the step everyone skips and later regrets

Before writing a single line of the Skill, run the task with Claude using no Skill at all. Save the output. Write down exactly what it got wrong, what you had to explain, and what you had to fix by hand.

Two reasons. First, those failures are your Skill's specification — you now know precisely what to close. Second, this is your before/after evidence for Step 9. Without it you will be presenting a feeling.

Step 4 · Identify the required context

Run the Unit 3 test on every candidate line. Keep only what is genuinely yours.

Cut it — knowledge

Definitions of standard terms. Explanations of why quality matters. How the general practice works. Encouragement to be thorough.

Keep it — context

Your templates. Your thresholds. Your naming conventions. The client's quirks. What broke last time. The rule everyone knows but nobody wrote down.

Step 5 · Define the Skill

Eight elements. Most are one line. Resist the urge to write a document.

Step 6 · Build it

Write the file. Use the Claude A / Claude B method from Unit 6 — draft with me, then test in a fresh session. Expect to delete a third of the first draft.

Where the file goes

Claude Code: ~/.claude/skills/<your-skill-name>/SKILL.md for personal use, or .claude/skills/<your-skill-name>/SKILL.md inside a project repo to share it with everyone who clones that repo.

claude.ai: zip the folder, upload via Settings → Features. API: upload through the Skills API — workspace-wide.

A rollout constraint to decide now, not in month two

Skills do not sync between surfaces, and on claude.ai custom Skills are per user — there is no admin distribution and no central management. For a 21-person rollout that matters enormously.

If you want one canonical version everyone gets: put Skills in a git repository and use Claude Code's project directory, or distribute through the API workspace. If people upload zips to claude.ai individually, you will have twenty-one diverging copies within a quarter. Decide this before the training, not after.

Step 7 · Test on real inputs

Your three evaluations from Unit 8. Real material, not invented examples.

Step 8 · Evaluate and improve

Score against Unit 8's dimensions. Be specific — "good" is not a score.

Step 9 · Demonstrate

The deliverable for your team session. Five minutes each, and the structure matters more than the polish:

  1. The task
    What you do, how often, how long it takes.
  2. Before
    The raw baseline output from Step 3. Show the actual failures.
  3. The Skill
    Show the file on screen. It is shorter than people expect — that is part of the lesson.
  4. After
    The same input, with the Skill. Point at the specific differences.
  5. What still needs a human
    Name the gate and why it is there. This is the part that earns senior trust, and the part most presenters leave out.
Coach your team on step 5

A demo that says "and it does the whole thing now" makes cautious people more cautious. A demo that says "it does these four steps, and a human still approves scope before anything reaches the client" makes them relax. Honesty about limits is what gets a rollout approved.
